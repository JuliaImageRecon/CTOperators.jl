Projection Geometries

A projection geometry describes where the rays run and the shape of the projection data. Detector geometries (cone beam with a flat or an arc detector, parallel beam) also describe the detector cells; ray lists hold arbitrary rays. See Set Up a Geometry for examples.

Interface

CTOperators.AbstractDetectorGeometry — Type
AbstractDetectorGeometry{T} <: AbstractProjectionGeometry{T}

Geometries of a source (or ray direction) and a flat or curved detector per view; the projection data has size (nu, nv, nviews). Besides the rays they describe the detector cells.

Interface (in addition to that of AbstractProjectionGeometry):

  • nviews(g), detectorsize(g) = (nu, nv)
  • subset(g, views): the geometry of a subset of the views
source
CTOperators.AbstractProjectionGeometry — Type
AbstractProjectionGeometry{T}

Supertype of acquisition geometries: where the rays run and the shape of the projection data. T is the element type of the stored coordinates.

Interface:

  • datasize(g): size of the projection data, e.g. (nu, nv, nviews)
  • ray(g, i) -> (a, b): ray i (linear index into the projection data) as two points SVector{3,T}; callable inside kernels
  • raytype(g): RaySegment() if the ray is the segment from a to b, RayLine() if it is the whole line through a and b

Geometries are moved to another device and converted to another element type with adapt of Adapt.jl (using Adapt), e.g. adapt(CuArray{Float32}, g). Lengths have no fixed unit; they must be in the unit of the ImageGeom they are used with.

source
CTOperators.RayLine — Type
RayLine()

raytype of geometries whose ray i is the whole line through the two points of ray(g, i), e.g. parallel beams. Only the part inside the volume contributes.

source
CTOperators.RaySegment — Type
RaySegment()

raytype of geometries whose ray i is the segment between the two points of ray(g, i), e.g. from the source to a detector pixel.

CTOperators.projection_geometry — Method
projection_geometry(g)

The projection geometry of CTOperators.jl that describes g: g itself for the geometries of this package; package extensions convert the geometries of other packages, e.g. those of Sinograms.jl (SinoPar, SinoFanFlat, SinoFanArc, CtPar, CtFanFlat, CtFanArc). The projectors call it, so they accept these geometries directly.

Geometry types

CTOperators.RayList — Type
RayList(starts, ends; datasize=(size(starts, 2),))

Arbitrary rays given by their start and end points (columns of the 3 × nrays matrices starts and ends). Ray i is the segment from starts[:, i] to ends[:, i]. datasize gives the projection data a shape, e.g. a sinogram: ray i is element i of the data array in column-major order.

RayList(g::AbstractProjectionGeometry)

The rays of a geometry with RaySegment rays, computed on the device of g (geometries with RayLine rays, such as parallel beams, have no end points and throw an error).

source
CTOperators.ConeBeam — Type
ConeBeam(source, center, u, v; nu, nv)

Cone beam with a flat detector of nu × nv cells and an arbitrary trajectory, given per view k (columns of 3 × nviews matrices):

  • source[:, k]: position of the source
  • center[:, k]: center of the detector
  • u[:, k], v[:, k]: vectors from one detector cell to the next along the rows (u) and the columns (v); their lengths are the cell sizes

The center of cell (iu, iv) is center + (iu − (nu + 1)/2) u + (iv − (nv + 1)/2) v, and ray (iu, iv, k) runs from the source to this point. The projection data has size (nu, nv, nviews).

circular_cone_beam and helical_cone_beam construct the common trajectories.

source
CTOperators.ArcConeBeam — Type
ArcConeBeam(source, center, u, v; nu, nv)

Cone beam with a curved detector of nu × nv cells on a cylinder about the source, as in clinical CT scanners, and an arbitrary trajectory, given per view k (columns of 3 × nviews matrices):

  • source[:, k]: position of the source, on the axis of the detector cylinder (the axis runs along v)
  • center[:, k]: center of the detector; its distance from the axis is the radius of the cylinder
  • u[:, k]: the direction of the detector rows at the center (tangent to the arc); its length is the cell size along the arc
  • v[:, k]: vector from one detector row to the next along the cylinder axis; its length is the cell size

Cell (iu, iv) sits at the fan angle γ = (iu − (nu + 1)/2) |u| / r from the center, with r the radius, and is offset by (iv − (nv + 1)/2) v along the cylinder axis; ray (iu, iv, k) runs from the source to it. The projection data has size (nu, nv, nviews). u must be orthogonal to v and to center − source (checked for CPU arrays).

circular_cone_beam and helical_cone_beam with detector=:arc construct the common trajectories.

source
CTOperators.ParallelBeam — Type
ParallelBeam(direction, center, u, v; nu, nv)

Parallel beam with a flat detector of nu × nv cells, given per view k (columns of 3 × nviews matrices):

  • direction[:, k]: direction of the rays (any nonzero length)
  • center[:, k]: the center of the detector; since the rays are whole lines, its position along direction does not matter
  • u[:, k], v[:, k]: vectors from one detector cell to the next along the rows (u) and the columns (v); their lengths are the cell sizes

Ray (iu, iv, k) is the whole line through the center of cell (iu, iv) (see ConeBeam) along direction[:, k] (RayLine). The projection data has size (nu, nv, nviews).

parallel_beam constructs the common rotation about the z axis.

source

Several rays per cell

A detector geometry wrapped with a point strategy (where in each cell the rays end) and a weighting strategy.

CTOperators.GaussianWeights — Type
GaussianWeights(sigma_u, sigma_v=sigma_u)

Subrays weighted by a Gaussian of the offset from the cell center, with standard deviations sigma_u, sigma_v in cell units: a detector response that falls off towards the cell edges. Weighting strategy of MultiRay.

source
CTOperators.JitteredPoints — Type
JitteredPoints(su, sv=su; seed=0)

The points of RegularPoints, each moved to a random position within its subcell ("jittered sampling", R. L. Cook, "Stochastic sampling in computer graphics", ACM Transactions on Graphics 5(1), 51-72, 1986). The positions are fixed by seed, the detector cell, the view and the subray, so they are the same in the forward and the back projection, on every device and in subset. Point strategy of MultiRay.

source
CTOperators.MultiRay — Type
MultiRay(geometry, points; weights=UniformWeights())

The detector geometry geometry (ConeBeam, ArcConeBeam, ParallelBeam) with several rays per detector cell. The rays of a cell end at the positions given by the point strategy points (RegularPoints, JitteredPoints, RandomPoints, Points) and are averaged with the weights of weights (UniformWeights, GaussianWeights), normalized to sum 1 per cell. A ray-driven projector built on it computes

y[i] = sum_k w_k * (line integral along subray k of data element i)

and the exact adjoint. This averages the line integrals over the cell (closer to what a detector cell measures than one line through its center) and removes the gaps between rays that occur when detector cells are large against the voxels. The data has the size of geometry.

For 2D images (one voxel layer along z) the projectors keep the subrays in the image plane: they are spread along u only, and point strategies with several subcells along v are rejected.

A MultiRay has no single ray per data element (no ray); projectors that trace only one ray per cell reject it. subset and adapt keep the points and weights; random positions stay the same for the views of a subset.

Example

g = MultiRay(circular_cone_beam(; angles, dso, dsd, nu, nv, du), JitteredPoints(2, 2; seed=1))
op = JosephProjector(image, g)

CTOperators.Points — Type
Points(offsets)

Given points, the same in every detector cell: column k of the 2 × K matrix offsets is the position of subray k in cell units, (0, 0) the cell center and ±1/2 the cell edges. Points outside the cell are allowed. Point strategy of MultiRay.

source
CTOperators.RandomPoints — Type
RandomPoints(n; seed=0)

n random points in each detector cell, fixed like those of JitteredPoints. Point strategy of MultiRay.

source
CTOperators.RegularPoints — Type
RegularPoints(su, sv=su)

The centers of su × sv subcells of each detector cell: su along u, sv along v. Point strategy of MultiRay.

source
CTOperators.UniformWeights — Type
UniformWeights()

All subrays of a detector cell count equally. Weighting strategy of MultiRay.

Trajectories

Constructors for the common scans.

CTOperators.circular_cone_beam — Method
circular_cone_beam(; angles, dso, dsd, nu, nv, du, dv=du, offset_u=0, offset_v=0, source_z=0,
                   detector=:flat)

Cone-beam geometry of a source on a circle of radius dso about the z axis and a detector at distance dsd from the source, nu × nv cells of size du × dv, one view per angle in angles (radians, source counterclockwise from the y axis). At angle β = 0 the source is on the +y axis, the detector rows run along +x and the columns along +z.

detector=:flat gives a flat detector (ConeBeam), detector=:arc a detector curved on a cylinder about the source (ArcConeBeam), with du the cell size along the arc at distance dsd (an angle of du / dsd per cell) and offset_u shifting the cells along the arc.

source_z is the height of source and detector center, a number or one value per view; with source_z varying the trajectory is no longer circular (see helical_cone_beam).

offset_u and offset_v shift the detector within its plane, in cells: the cell positions along u are ((0:nu-1) .- (nu - 1)/2 .- offset_u) .* du, so a positive offset moves the detector towards −u (−v). Lengths are in the unit of the ImageGeom the geometry is used with.

source
CTOperators.helical_cone_beam — Method
helical_cone_beam(; angles, pitch, z0=0, kwargs...)

ConeBeam geometry (with detector=:arc an ArcConeBeam) of a helical trajectory: as circular_cone_beam (same keywords), with source and detector moving along z by pitch (a length) per turn, z = z0 + pitch (β − β₁)/2π for view angle β (β₁ = angles[1]).

An object that translates along the rotation axis with speed s during a circular scan sees this geometry with pitch = −s · (time per turn).

source
CTOperators.parallel_beam — Method
parallel_beam(; angles, nu, nv, du, dv=du, offset_u=0, offset_v=0)

ParallelBeam geometry rotating about the z axis, nu × nv cells of size du × dv, one view per angle in angles (radians). At angle ϕ the rays run along (−sin ϕ, cos ϕ, 0) and the detector rows along (cos ϕ, sin ϕ, 0); the detector is centered on the rotation axis, its columns along +z. This matches the parallel-beam coordinates (u, v, ϕ) of radon in ImagePhantoms.jl.

offset_u and offset_v shift the detector within its plane, in cells: the cell positions along u are ((0:nu-1) .- (nu - 1)/2 .- offset_u) .* du, so a positive offset moves the detector towards −u (−v). Lengths are in the unit of the ImageGeom the geometry is used with.
