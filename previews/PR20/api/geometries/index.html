Projection Geometries

A projection geometry describes where the rays run and the shape of the projection data. Detector geometries (cone beam with a flat or an arc detector, parallel beam) also describe the detector cells; ray lists hold arbitrary rays. See Set Up a Geometry for examples.

Interface

CTOperators.AbstractDetectorGeometry — Type
AbstractDetectorGeometry{T} <: AbstractProjectionGeometry{T}

Geometries of a source (or ray direction) and a flat or curved detector per view; the projection data has size (nu, nv, nviews). Besides the rays they describe the detector cells.

Interface (in addition to that of AbstractProjectionGeometry):

  • nviews(g), detectorsize(g) = (nu, nv)
  • subset(g, views): the geometry of a subset of the views
  • cell_ray(g, cu, cv, k), for several rays per cell
source
CTOperators.AbstractProjectionGeometry — Type
AbstractProjectionGeometry{T}

Supertype of acquisition geometries: where the rays run and the shape of the projection data. T is the element type of the stored coordinates.

Interface:

  • datasize(g): size of the projection data, e.g. (nu, nv, nviews)
  • ray(g, i) -> (a, b): ray i (linear index into the projection data) as two points SVector{3,T}; callable inside kernels
  • raytype(g): RaySegment() if the ray is the segment from a to b, RayLine() if it is the whole line through a and b

Geometries are moved to another device and converted to another element type with adapt of Adapt.jl (using Adapt), e.g. adapt(CuArray{Float32}, g). Lengths have no fixed unit; they must be in the unit of the ImageGeom they are used with.

source
CTOperators.DetectorHit — Type
DetectorHit{T}

Where a point projects onto the detector, the result of project_point. Fields:

  • cu, cv: the position in cell units as in cell_ray (integers are cell centers), so that cell_ray(g, cu, cv, k) runs through the point.
  • density: (R / r)² / (a cos φ), with R the distance from the source to the detector point, r that to p (R / r = 1 for parallel beams), a the area of a cell and φ the angle between the ray and the detector normal there. A point mass m at p adds m * density to the means of the line integrals over the cells around (cu, cv), summed over these cells (derived on the page Voxel-Driven Method).
  • magnification: R / r.
  • rowsize: the spacing of the detector rows (dv, the length of the geometry vector v).
  • ok: whether the ray meets the detector in front of the source (cone beams) or at all (rays parallel to a flat detector do not).
source
CTOperators.RayLine — Type
RayLine()

raytype of geometries whose ray i is the whole line through the two points of ray(g, i), e.g. parallel beams. Only the part inside the volume contributes.

source
CTOperators.RaySegment — Type
RaySegment()

raytype of geometries whose ray i is the segment between the two points of ray(g, i), e.g. from the source to a detector cell.

source
CTOperators.cell_ray — Function
cell_ray(g::AbstractDetectorGeometry, cu, cv, k) -> (a, b)

The ray of view k that ends at position (cu, cv) on the detector, as two points like ray: for cone beams the source and the detector point, for parallel beams the detector point and that point plus the direction. Positions are in cell units: integers are cell centers, so cell_ray(g, iu, iv, k) is the ray of cell (iu, iv); half-integers are cell edges, e.g. cell_ray(g, iu + 1/2, iv, k) runs through the middle of the edge between cells iu and iu + 1. Callable inside kernels. Detector sampling in a projector's kernel needs it from the geometry.

source
CTOperators.datasize — Function
datasize(g::AbstractProjectionGeometry)

Size of the projection data.

source
CTOperators.detectorsize — Function
detectorsize(g::AbstractDetectorGeometry) -> (nu, nv)

Number of detector cells along u (a detector row) and along v (a detector column).

source
CTOperators.nrays — Method
nrays(g::AbstractProjectionGeometry)

Number of data elements, prod(datasize(g)): one ray each, or several rays per cell with detector sampling (see sampled).

source
CTOperators.nviews — Function
nviews(g::AbstractDetectorGeometry)

Number of views, the last dimension of the projection data.

source
CTOperators.project_point — Function
project_point(g::AbstractDetectorGeometry, p, k) -> DetectorHit

Where the ray of view k through the point p (world coordinates) meets the detector, as a DetectorHit: the inverse of cell_ray. Implemented for ConeBeam, ArcConeBeam and ParallelBeam; callable inside kernels.

source
CTOperators.projection_geometry — Method
projection_geometry(g)

The projection geometry of CTOperators.jl that describes g: g itself for the geometries of this package; package extensions convert the geometries of other packages, e.g. those of Sinograms.jl (SinoPar, SinoFanFlat, SinoFanArc, CtPar, CtFanFlat, CtFanArc). The projectors call it, so they accept these geometries directly.

source
CTOperators.ray — Function
ray(g::AbstractProjectionGeometry, i) -> (a, b)

Ray i (linear index into the projection data) as two points SVector{3,T}, see raytype. Callable inside kernels.

source
CTOperators.raytype — Function
raytype(g::AbstractProjectionGeometry)

RaySegment() or RayLine().

source
CTOperators.storage_array — Function
storage_array(g::AbstractProjectionGeometry)

One of the arrays the geometry stores, which tells its device (KA.get_backend(storage_array(g))) and array type. Every geometry type implements it.

source
CTOperators.subset — Function
subset(g::AbstractDetectorGeometry, views)

The geometry of the views views of g (a vector or range of view indices; one view: k:k), for ordered-subset methods. subset(op, views) gives the projector of such a subset.

Geometry types

CTOperators.RayList — Type
RayList(starts, ends; datasize=(size(starts, 2),))

Arbitrary rays given by their start and end points (columns of the 3 × nrays matrices starts and ends). Ray i is the segment from starts[:, i] to ends[:, i]. datasize gives the projection data a shape, e.g. a sinogram: ray i is element i of the data array in column-major order.

RayList(g::AbstractProjectionGeometry)

The rays of a geometry with RaySegment rays, computed on the device of g (geometries with RayLine rays, such as parallel beams, have no end points and throw an error).

source
CTOperators.ConeBeam — Type
ConeBeam(source, center, u, v; nu, nv)

Cone beam with a flat detector of nu × nv cells and an arbitrary trajectory, given per view k (columns of 3 × nviews matrices):

  • source[:, k]: position of the source
  • center[:, k]: center of the detector
  • u[:, k], v[:, k]: vectors from one detector cell to the next along the rows (u) and the columns (v); their lengths are the cell sizes

The center of cell (iu, iv) is center + (iu − (nu + 1)/2) u + (iv − (nv + 1)/2) v, and ray (iu, iv, k) runs from the source to this point. The projection data has size (nu, nv, nviews).

circular_cone_beam and helical_cone_beam construct the common trajectories.

source
CTOperators.ArcConeBeam — Type
ArcConeBeam(source, center, u, v; nu, nv)

Cone beam with a curved detector of nu × nv cells on a cylinder about the source, as in clinical CT scanners, and an arbitrary trajectory, given per view k (columns of 3 × nviews matrices):

  • source[:, k]: position of the source, on the axis of the detector cylinder (the axis runs along v)
  • center[:, k]: center of the detector; its distance from the axis is the radius of the cylinder
  • u[:, k]: the direction of the detector rows at the center (tangent to the arc); its length is the cell size along the arc
  • v[:, k]: vector from one detector row to the next along the cylinder axis; its length is the cell size

Cell (iu, iv) sits at the fan angle γ = (iu − (nu + 1)/2) |u| / r from the center, with r the radius, and is offset by (iv − (nv + 1)/2) v along the cylinder axis; ray (iu, iv, k) runs from the source to it. The projection data has size (nu, nv, nviews). u must be orthogonal to v and to center − source (checked for CPU arrays).

circular_cone_beam and helical_cone_beam with detector=:arc construct the common trajectories.

source
CTOperators.ParallelBeam — Type
ParallelBeam(direction, center, u, v; nu, nv)

Parallel beam with a flat detector of nu × nv cells, given per view k (columns of 3 × nviews matrices):

  • direction[:, k]: direction of the rays (any nonzero length)
  • center[:, k]: the center of the detector; since the rays are whole lines, its position along direction does not matter
  • u[:, k], v[:, k]: vectors from one detector cell to the next along the rows (u) and the columns (v); their lengths are the cell sizes

Ray (iu, iv, k) is the whole line through the center of cell (iu, iv) (see ConeBeam) along direction[:, k] (RayLine). The projection data has size (nu, nv, nviews).

parallel_beam constructs the common rotation about the z axis.

Point and weighting strategies

Point strategies (where the rays of a sample end in a detector cell or start in a focal spot) and weighting strategies, together with the functions that custom strategies implement (see Write a Sampling Strategy).

CTOperators.AbstractPoints — Type
AbstractPoints{K}

Supertype of the point strategies of DetectorSampling and FocalSpot: where the K rays of a detector cell end, or where the rays of a focal spot start. A subtype implements CTOperators.point_offset. If it stores floating-point numbers, it also implements CTOperators.points_eltype, so that it follows the element type of the projector. The type can live in any package; see Write a Sampling Strategy.

source
CTOperators.AbstractWeights — Type
AbstractWeights

Supertype of the weighting strategies of DetectorSampling and FocalSpot. A subtype implements CTOperators.subray_weight, and CTOperators.weights_eltype if it stores floating-point numbers. The type can live in any package; see Write a Sampling Strategy.

source
CTOperators.DiskPoints — Type
DiskPoints(rings, angles=6)

Points inside the disk of radius 1/2 about the center: rings rings of equal area with angles points each, rings * angles points, the same in every cell and view. The point of ring i and angle j sits at radius sqrt((i - 1/2) / rings) / 2 and angle 2π (j - 1/2) / angles. For a circular focal spot (FocalSpot); also a point strategy of DetectorSampling.

source
CTOperators.GaussianPoints — Type
GaussianPoints(n, sigma_u, sigma_v=sigma_u; seed=0)

n random points per detector cell, normally distributed around the cell center with standard deviations sigma_u, sigma_v in cell units: a detector response set by where the rays land, with all rays counting equally. Points can land outside the cell, which models blur across neighboring cells. The offsets are fixed like those of JitteredPoints. Point strategy of DetectorSampling and FocalSpot.

source
CTOperators.GaussianWeights — Type
GaussianWeights(sigma_u, sigma_v=sigma_u)

Rays of a cell weighted by a Gaussian of the offset from the cell center, with standard deviations sigma_u, sigma_v in cell units: a detector response that falls off toward the cell edges. Weighting strategy of DetectorSampling and FocalSpot.

source
CTOperators.JitteredPoints — Type
JitteredPoints(su, sv=su; seed=0)

The points of RegularPoints, each moved to a random position within its subcell ("jittered sampling", R. L. Cook, "Stochastic sampling in computer graphics", ACM Transactions on Graphics 5(1), 51-72, 1986). The offsets are fixed by seed (default 0), the detector cell, the view and the ray, so they are the same in the forward and the back projection, on every device and in subset. Point strategy of DetectorSampling and FocalSpot.

source
CTOperators.Points — Type
Points(offsets)

Given points, the same in every detector cell: column k of the 2 × K matrix offsets is the offset of ray k in cell units, (0, 0) the cell center and ±1/2 the cell edges. Points outside the cell are allowed. Point strategy of DetectorSampling and FocalSpot.

source
CTOperators.RandomPoints — Type
RandomPoints(n; seed=0)

n random points, uniformly distributed in each detector cell, fixed like those of JitteredPoints. Point strategy of DetectorSampling and FocalSpot.

source
CTOperators.RegularPoints — Type
RegularPoints(su, sv=su)

The centers of su × sv subcells of each detector cell, su along u and sv along v: su * sv rays per cell. Point strategy of DetectorSampling and FocalSpot.

source
CTOperators.UniformWeights — Type
UniformWeights()

All rays of a detector cell count equally. Weighting strategy of DetectorSampling and FocalSpot.

source
CTOperators.in_plane — Method
in_plane(geometry)

geometry with the rays of a CTOperators.CellSampled geometry in the image plane (offsets along u only); other geometries unchanged. The constructor and similar_projector of a projector call it for images one voxel thick in z, see CTOperators.one_layer.

source
CTOperators.one_layer — Method
one_layer(image::ImageGeom)

Whether image is one voxel thick in z: a 2D image, or a 3D image with nz = 1. The rays of a cell stay in its plane: Joseph's method weights rays away from the center of the layer down, and a tilted ray may leave the layer.

source
CTOperators.point_offset — Function
point_offset(points, T, iu, iv, view, k) -> (du, dv)

The offset of ray k (from 1 to K) from the center of the detector cell (iu, iv) in view view, in element type T and in cell units (±1/2 are the cell edges). view is numbered as in the full geometry, so that subsets draw the same random offsets. Called in kernels: no allocations. Fields of a strategy must be plain values: they reach the kernels unchanged, so an array field would stay on the CPU.

source
CTOperators.points_eltype — Method
points_eltype(points, T)

points with the floating-point numbers it stores in element type T. The default returns points unchanged, for strategies that store none.

source
CTOperators.random_pair — Method
random_pair(T, seed, iu, iv, view, k) -> (ru, rv)

Two uniform random numbers in [0, 1), fixed by the arguments: the same on every device and in every call, and independent for different arguments. seed is a UInt32, (iu, iv) the detector cell, view the view; k is any number, usually the ray of the cell. For random point strategies.

source
CTOperators.subray_weight — Function
subray_weight(weights, du, dv)

The ray weight of a ray at offset (du, dv) from the cell center, not normalized: the kernel divides by the sum of the ray weights of the cell, which must not be zero. Called in kernels: no allocations.

source
CTOperators.varies_per_cell — Method
varies_per_cell(points::AbstractPoints) -> Bool

Whether the offsets of points depend on the detector cell and the view (true for random positions). Sampling that runs sequentially, as one projector per offset (see sampled), needs offsets that do not: it shifts a whole geometry. true for custom strategies unless they define a method.

source
CTOperators.weights_eltype — Method
weights_eltype(weights, T)

weights with the floating-point numbers it stores in element type T. The default returns weights unchanged, for strategies that store none.

source

Sampling of the beam

The samples of a focal spot and of the detector cells, for sampled and stacked, and the geometry of a sample.

CTOperators.AbstractSampling — Type
AbstractSampling

Supertype of the sampling descriptions of sampled and stacked: FocalSpot (the source side) and DetectorSampling (the detector side).

source
CTOperators.DetectorSampling — Type
DetectorSampling(points; weights=UniformWeights())

Several rays per detector cell, ending at the offsets of points (in cell units, ±1/2 at the cell edges) and averaged with the ray weights of weights, normalized to sum 1 per cell.

source
CTOperators.FocalSpot — Type
FocalSpot(fu, fv=fu; points, weights=UniformWeights())

A focal spot of size fu × fv (in the length unit of the image): a flat patch through the source, parallel to a flat detector (ConeBeam) or perpendicular to the central ray for a detector on an arc (ArcConeBeam), with fu along the detector rows (u) and fv along the columns (v). The rays of a view start at the points of points (offsets in units of the spot size, ±1/2 at its edges: RegularPoints for a rectangle, DiskPoints for a circle or ellipse, or any AbstractPoints evaluated once per view), weighted with weights (UniformWeights, GaussianWeights in the same units, or any AbstractWeights), normalized to sum 1 per view.

The keyword points has no default: one point at the center would be a point source.

For images one voxel thick in z the spot is sampled along u only: RegularPoints and JitteredPoints need one row (RegularPoints(n, 1)), DiskPoints are rejected, other strategies get offsets along v of 0.

source
CTOperators.Shifted — Type
Shifted(geometry, source, detector)

geometry (an ArcConeBeam, or another detector geometry without a method of shift of its own) with the rays starting at source[:, k] added to its source in view k (a 3 × nviews matrix of lengths, or nothing) and ending at the detector position shifted by detector[:, k] cells along u and v (a 2 × nviews matrix, or nothing). The detector does not move with the source. Built by sampled for the samples of a focal spot or of the detector cells; for ConeBeam and ParallelBeam it builds plain geometries instead.

source

Trajectories

Constructors for the common scans.

CTOperators.circular_cone_beam — Method
circular_cone_beam(; angles, dso, dsd, nu, nv, du, dv=du, offset_u=0, offset_v=0, source_z=0,
                   detector=:flat)

Cone-beam geometry of a source on a circle of radius dso about the z axis and a detector at distance dsd from the source, nu × nv cells of size du × dv, one view per angle in angles (radians, source counterclockwise from the y axis). At angle β = 0 the source is on the +y axis, the detector rows run along +x and the columns along +z.

detector=:flat gives a flat detector (ConeBeam), detector=:arc a detector curved on a cylinder about the source (ArcConeBeam), with du the cell size along the arc at distance dsd (an angle of du / dsd per cell) and offset_u shifting the cells along the arc.

source_z is the height of source and detector center, a number or one value per view; with source_z varying the trajectory is no longer circular (see helical_cone_beam).

offset_u and offset_v shift the detector within its plane, in cells: the cell positions along u are ((0:nu-1) .- (nu - 1)/2 .- offset_u) .* du, so a positive offset moves the detector toward −u (−v). Lengths are in the unit of the ImageGeom the geometry is used with.

source
CTOperators.helical_cone_beam — Method
helical_cone_beam(; angles, pitch, z0=0, kwargs...)

ConeBeam geometry (with detector=:arc an ArcConeBeam) of a helical trajectory: as circular_cone_beam (same keywords), with source and detector moving along z by pitch (a length) per turn, z = z0 + pitch (β − β₁)/2π for view angle β (β₁ = angles[1]).

An object that translates along the rotation axis with speed s during a circular scan sees this geometry with pitch = −s · (time per turn).

source
CTOperators.parallel_beam — Method
parallel_beam(; angles, nu, nv, du, dv=du, offset_u=0, offset_v=0)

ParallelBeam geometry rotating about the z axis, nu × nv cells of size du × dv, one view per angle in angles (radians). At angle ϕ the rays run along (−sin ϕ, cos ϕ, 0) and the detector rows along (cos ϕ, sin ϕ, 0); the detector is centered on the rotation axis, its columns along +z. This matches the parallel-beam coordinates (u, v, ϕ) of radon in ImagePhantoms.jl.

offset_u and offset_v shift the detector within its plane, in cells: the cell positions along u are ((0:nu-1) .- (nu - 1)/2 .- offset_u) .* du, so a positive offset moves the detector toward −u (−v). Lengths are in the unit of the ImageGeom the geometry is used with.
