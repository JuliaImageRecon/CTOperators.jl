Projectors

The projectors of the package, all with an exact adjoint. Siddon and Joseph trace the rays of a geometry through the voxel grid, one per detector cell or several with detector sampling (sampled); Siddon's Method and Joseph's Method explain how they weight the voxels. The distance-driven method integrates over the area of each detector cell, and the voxel-driven method projects each voxel onto the detector; both need a detector geometry. System Matrix describes what they have in common.

Siddon

CTOperators.SiddonProjector — Type
SiddonProjector(image::ImageGeom, geometry; eltype=Float32, storage=Array, launch=nothing, raymask=nothing)

Projector with Siddon's method: the weight of a voxel for a ray is the exact length of the ray inside the voxel (Siddon 1985), computed incrementally as in Jacobs et al. 1998. Works with every AbstractProjectionGeometry. The back projection is the exact adjoint.

Keywords:

  • eltype is the element type of the computation and of the stored geometry.
  • storage is the array type that selects the device (Array, CuArray, ROCArray, …). Image and projection data passed to the operator must live on that device.
  • launch is a LaunchConfig to tune the kernel launches for a device, nothing for the defaults of the device.
  • raymask is nothing or an array with one entry per data element, of the data size (raymask[u, v, view]) or a vector, on any device: the data elements that are used (true). It is stored as Bool on the device of the operator. Masked data elements give 0 in the forward projection and contribute nothing to the back projection.

The mask of image (e.g. ImageGeom(MaskCircle(); dims, deltas), the circular field of view) selects the voxels: voxels outside count as 0 in the forward projection and get 0 in the back projection, and the rays are only traced through the bounding box of the image mask. See Use Masks.

Example

image = ImageGeom(; dims=(128, 128, 64), deltas=(1, 1, 1))
geometry = circular_cone_beam(; angles=range(0, 2pi, 361)[1:360], dso=500, dsd=1000,
                              nu=256, nv=128, du=1, dv=1)
op = SiddonProjector(image, geometry)
x = rand(Float32, 128, 128, 64)
y = op * x                 # forward projection, size (256, 128, 360)
z = adjoint(op) * y        # back projection

References

  • R. L. Siddon, "Fast calculation of the exact radiological path for a three-dimensional CT array", Medical Physics 12(2), 252–255, 1985.
  • F. Jacobs, E. Sundermann, B. De Sutter, M. Christiaens, I. Lemahieu, "A fast algorithm to calculate the exact radiological path through a pixel or voxel space", J. Comput. Inf. Technol. 6(1), 89–94, 1998.

Joseph

CTOperators.JosephProjector — Type
JosephProjector(image::ImageGeom, geometry; eltype=Float32, storage=Array, launch=nothing, raymask=nothing)

Projector with Joseph's method (Joseph 1982): the image is interpolated bilinearly within each layer of voxels perpendicular to the axis the ray runs most along (its principal axis), and the line integral is the sum over the layers of the interpolated value times the length of the ray in the layer. Compared with SiddonProjector:

  • the image model is smooth, so the projection changes continuously when a ray moves;
  • for one parallel-beam view at 17° with cells as wide as the voxels, the rays cover the voxels more evenly (see Joseph's Method); at exactly 45° both methods give the same weights (Joseph 1982);
  • the projection is exact for images that are linear functions of the position rather than for voxel images.

Works with every AbstractProjectionGeometry. The back projection is the exact adjoint.

Outside the volume the image is zero; across the ray it fades to zero up to half a voxel beyond the faces of the volume. If a segment starts or ends inside the volume, only the part of the end layer it actually crosses is counted.

Keywords:

  • eltype is the element type of the computation and of the stored geometry.
  • storage is the array type that selects the device (Array, CuArray, ROCArray, …). Image and projection data passed to the operator must live on that device.
  • launch is a LaunchConfig to tune the kernel launches for a device, nothing for the defaults of the device.
  • raymask is nothing or an array with one entry per data element, of the data size (raymask[u, v, view]) or a vector, on any device: the data elements that are used (true). It is stored as Bool on the device of the operator. Masked data elements give 0 in the forward projection and contribute nothing to the back projection.

The mask of image (e.g. ImageGeom(MaskCircle(); dims, deltas), the circular field of view) selects the voxels: voxels outside count as 0 in the forward projection and get 0 in the back projection, and the rays are only traced through the bounding box of the image mask. See Use Masks.

Example

image = ImageGeom(; dims=(128, 128, 64), deltas=(1, 1, 1))
geometry = circular_cone_beam(; angles=range(0, 2pi, 361)[1:360], dso=500, dsd=1000,
                              nu=256, nv=128, du=1, dv=1)
op = JosephProjector(image, geometry)
x = rand(Float32, 128, 128, 64)
y = op * x                 # forward projection, size (256, 128, 360)
z = adjoint(op) * y        # back projection

References

  • P. M. Joseph, "An improved algorithm for reprojecting rays through pixel images", IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging 1(3), 192–196, 1982.

Distance-Driven

CTOperators.DistanceDrivenProjector — Type
DistanceDrivenProjector(image::ImageGeom, geometry; eltype=Float32, storage=Array, launch=nothing, raymask=nothing)

Projector with the distance-driven method (De Man & Basu 2004), which integrates over the detector cells instead of following single rays. Per view the image is cut into layers perpendicular to the principal axis of the view, the axis along which the view's central ray (from the source through the detector center) advances most. In every layer, the edge rays through the midpoints of the four edges of a detector cell span a rectangle, the cell's footprint; the cell measures the mean of the image over the footprint times the length per layer of its ray, summed over the layers. The footprints of neighboring cells share their edges and cover each layer without gaps. The back projection is the exact adjoint. See Distance-Driven Method and System Matrix.

Works with detector geometries (AbstractDetectorGeometry: ConeBeam, ArcConeBeam, ParallelBeam), not with ray lists, which have no detector cells, nor with detector sampling (sampled): the footprint already covers the whole cell. For a 2D image the footprint extends only within the image plane.

Keywords:

  • eltype is the element type of the computation and of the stored geometry.
  • storage is the array type that selects the device (Array, CuArray, ROCArray, …). Image and projection data passed to the operator must live on that device.
  • launch is a LaunchConfig to tune the kernel launches for a device, nothing for the defaults of the device.
  • raymask is nothing or an array with one entry per data element (detector cell and view), of the data size (raymask[u, v, view]) or a vector, on any device: the cells that are used (true). It is stored as Bool on the device of the operator. Masked data elements give 0 in the forward projection and contribute nothing to the back projection.

The mask of image (e.g. ImageGeom(MaskCircle(); dims, deltas), the circular field of view) selects the voxels: voxels outside count as 0 in the forward projection and get 0 in the back projection, and the layers are limited to the bounding box of the image mask. See Use Masks.

Example

image = ImageGeom(; dims=(128, 128, 64), deltas=(1, 1, 1))
geometry = circular_cone_beam(; angles=range(0, 2pi, 361)[1:360], dso=500, dsd=1000,
                              nu=256, nv=128, du=1, dv=1)
op = DistanceDrivenProjector(image, geometry)
x = rand(Float32, 128, 128, 64)
y = op * x                 # forward projection, size (256, 128, 360)
z = adjoint(op) * y        # back projection

References

  • B. De Man, S. Basu, "Distance-driven projection and backprojection in three dimensions", Physics in Medicine and Biology 49(11), 2463–2475, 2004.
  • R. Liu, L. Fu, B. De Man, H. Yu, "GPU-based branchless distance-driven projection and backprojection", IEEE Transactions on Computational Imaging 3(4), 617–632, 2017.

Voxel-Driven

The projector and its scales: how the bilinear weights of a voxel are scaled per view (see Voxel-Driven Method).

CTOperators.AbstractVoxelScale — Type
AbstractVoxelScale

How a VoxelDrivenProjector scales the bilinear weights of a voxel in a view: the weight of voxel j in cell i is voxel_scale(scale, geometry, k, grid, hit) times the bilinear weight of cell i at the point where voxel j projects (hit, from project_point). Subtypes: LineIntegralScale (the default) and UnitScale. A new scale is a subtype with a method of voxel_scale; it must be usable inside kernels: isbits, or with arrays and an Adapt.adapt_structure method (adapt of the projector moves the scale with it). A scale that depends on the view k needs a method of subset(scale, views) that returns the scale of the views views, for subset of the projector.

source
CTOperators.LineIntegralScale — Type
LineIntegralScale()

The default scale of a VoxelDrivenProjector: the forward projection approximates the mean of the line integrals over each detector cell, the detector model of the distance-driven method. A voxel of volume V projecting with density ρ (see project_point) gets the scale V ρ: its forward projection, integrated over the detector area, is V (R / r)² / cos φ, what the line integrals through a point mass V give (when all its cells lie on the detector). See Voxel-Driven Method for the derivation.

For a 2D image (one voxel thick in z) the line integrals run within the image plane: the scale is A (R / r) / (Δu cos φ) with A the pixel area and Δu the cell width, and every detector row whose ray passes within the thickness of the image at the position of the pixel gets the full weight (no averaging along v).

source
CTOperators.UnitScale — Type
UnitScale()

A scale of 1 for a VoxelDrivenProjector: the weights are the bilinear interpolation weights alone, without the voxel volume, the magnification or the obliquity of the ray. The forward projection then no longer approximates line integrals; the back projection is plain interpolation of the data, for methods that apply their own weights.

source
CTOperators.VoxelDrivenProjector — Type
VoxelDrivenProjector(image::ImageGeom, geometry; scale=LineIntegralScale(), eltype=Float32, storage=Array, launch=nothing, raymask=nothing)

Projector with the voxel-driven method (the pixel-driven method of De Man & Basu 2004): in every view, the center of each voxel is projected from the source (along the direction for parallel beams) onto the detector, and the voxel contributes to the 2 × 2 cells around that point with bilinear weights, times a scale (keyword scale, an AbstractVoxelScale). The back projection is the exact adjoint, the way implementations of filtered back projection usually back project, with the weights of the scale. See Voxel-Driven Method and System Matrix. Compared with the ray-driven projectors:

  • the back projection runs one thread per run of voxels along x and writes each voxel once, without atomic additions, so repeated back projections are equal; every voxel is covered, whatever the cell size;
  • the forward projection adds into the data with atomic additions, and with cells (at the object) smaller than the voxels it shows a pattern at the scale of the cells, the high-frequency artifacts of De Man & Basu (2004).

For a 2D image the cells are interpolated along u only: every detector row whose ray passes within the thickness of the image at the position of a pixel gets two cells with the full weight.

Works with detector geometries (AbstractDetectorGeometry: ConeBeam, ArcConeBeam, ParallelBeam), not with ray lists, which have no detector to project onto. Detector sampling (sampled) runs one projector per sample.

Keywords:

  • scale: LineIntegralScale (the default: the forward projection approximates line integrals, like the other projectors), UnitScale, or a scale of your own.
  • eltype is the element type of the computation and of the stored geometry.
  • storage is the array type that selects the device (Array, CuArray, ROCArray, …). Image and projection data passed to the operator must live on that device.
  • launch is a LaunchConfig to tune the kernel launches for a device, nothing for the defaults of the device; its layout is () or (R,), runs of R voxels along x per thread.
  • raymask is nothing or an array with one entry per data element (detector cell and view), of the data size (raymask[u, v, view]) or a vector, on any device: the cells that are used (true). It is stored as Bool on the device of the operator. Masked data elements give 0 in the forward projection and contribute nothing to the back projection.

The mask of image (e.g. ImageGeom(MaskCircle(); dims, deltas), the circular field of view) selects the voxels: voxels outside count as 0 in the forward projection and get 0 in the back projection; the threads cover the bounding box of the mask. See Use Masks.

Example

image = ImageGeom(; dims=(128, 128, 64), deltas=(1, 1, 1))
geometry = circular_cone_beam(; angles=range(0, 2pi, 361)[1:360], dso=500, dsd=1000,
                              nu=256, nv=128, du=1, dv=1)
op = VoxelDrivenProjector(image, geometry)
x = rand(Float32, 128, 128, 64)
y = op * x                 # forward projection, size (256, 128, 360)
z = adjoint(op) * y        # back projection

References

  • B. De Man, S. Basu, "Distance-driven projection and backprojection in three dimensions", Physics in Medicine and Biology 49(11), 2463–2475, 2004 (the pixel-driven method, its weight factors and artifacts).
  • W. Zhuang, S. S. Gopal, T. J. Hebert, "Numerical evaluation of methods for computing tomographic projections", IEEE Transactions on Nuclear Science 41(4), 1660–1665, 1994.
source

FDK

Reconstruction in one pass for circular scans, with the voxel-driven back projection (see Reconstruct with FDK and Filtered Back Projection), and the redundancy weighting: how lines measured more than once are weighted.

CTOperators.FDK — Type
FDK(image::ImageGeom, geometry; redundancy=FullScan(), window=:hann, cutoff=1, eltype=Float32, storage=Array,
    launch=nothing)

Reconstruction with the method of Feldkamp, Davis & Kress (1984) for a circular cone-beam scan (ConeBeam or ArcConeBeam with the source on a circle about the rotation axis), and filtered back projection for a ParallelBeam: the data are weighted, the detector rows filtered with the ramp filter and the result back projected with the voxel-driven method (see VoxelDrivenProjector; Filtered Back Projection explains the steps). The plan holds the filter, the FFT plans and the buffers; plan * y and mul!(x, plan, y) reconstruct the image from the line integrals y (of the data size of geometry). CTOperators.fdk(image, geometry, y) does it in one call.

The geometry must be a circular scan: the rotation axis runs along the detector columns (v) in all views, the rows (u) are perpendicular to it, the sources lie on a circle about the axis in a plane perpendicular to it, a flat detector is perpendicular to the central ray (the ray from the source to the axis), the cells have the same width in all views, and the views are in order along the circle; geometries that break these give an ArgumentError (helical scans, for example). How much of the circle the views must cover depends on the redundancy weighting: with FullScan, the default, the whole circle (parallel beams: half or the whole circle), with ParkerWeights half the circle plus the fan angle; the angle steps may differ between views. Whether the detector covers the object in every view is not checked: data of an object that reaches beyond the rays of the detector give a wrong image. Sinograms.jl geometries are converted with projection_geometry.

Keywords:

  • redundancy: how lines measured more than once are weighted, an AbstractRedundancy: FullScan (the default), ParkerWeights (short scans), ArrayRedundancy (weights as arrays) or a weighting of your own.
  • window: the window of the ramp filter, :hann (the default), :ramp (none), :hamming, :cosine, :shepplogan, or a function w(f) of the frequency f as a fraction of the cutoff. A window lowers the ramp at high frequencies (see Reconstruct with FDK and Write FDK Windows and Weights).
  • cutoff: the highest frequency passed, a fraction of the Nyquist frequency of the cells (0 < cutoff ≤ 1).
  • eltype, storage, launch: as for the projectors (the element type of the computation, the array type that selects the device, the launch of the back projection: a LaunchConfig with the layout of the VoxelDrivenProjector, () or (R,)).

The image mask of image selects the voxels that are reconstructed. An image with one voxel along z (2D, or 3D with nz = 1) needs a detector with one row. A plan owns its buffers: it must not run on several tasks at the same time. adapt(storage, plan) (Adapt.jl) moves a plan to another device, with new buffers and FFT plans.

Example

image = ImageGeom(; dims=(128, 128, 64), deltas=(1, 1, 1))
geometry = circular_cone_beam(; angles=range(0, 2pi, 361)[1:360], dso=500, dsd=1000,
                              nu=256, nv=128, du=1, dv=1)
plan = FDK(image, geometry)
x = plan * y               # y: line integrals of size (256, 128, 360)

References

  • L. A. Feldkamp, L. C. Davis, J. W. Kress, "Practical cone-beam algorithm", Journal of the Optical Society of America A 1(6), 612–619, 1984.

See also

  • A. C. Kak, M. Slaney, "Principles of Computerized Tomographic Imaging", IEEE Press, 1988, chapter 3.
source

CTOperators.AbstractRedundancy — Type
AbstractRedundancy

How FDK combines the measurements of lines that a scan measures more than once (keyword redundancy). A full scan measures every line twice, from opposite sides; other scans measure some lines once and some twice, and need weights that depend on the ray. Subtypes: FullScan (the default), ParkerWeights (short scans) and ArrayRedundancy (weights given as arrays).

A redundancy weighting checks whether it can reconstruct a scan, and gives two kinds of weights: a weight per data element (cell and view), applied before the ramp filter, and a weight per view, applied in the back projection with the angle step. Interface:

  • redundancy_weights(r, scan, geometry) -> (weight, views), on the host: weight is the object that the kernel passes to redundancy_weight (isbits, or with arrays and an Adapt.adapt_structure method; it is moved to the device with adapt(storage, weight)), and views a vector with the weight of each view. Throws an ArgumentError for scans it cannot handle.
  • redundancy_weight(weight, geometry, iu, iv, k): the weight of cell (iu, iv) in view k; called in kernels.

FDK back projects view k (the data times the cosine weight and weight, filtered with the ramp) with the factor views[k] · dso_k / dsd_k times the squared magnification of the voxel. The weights must split every line among its measurements: the product of weight and views[k], divided by the angle step of the view, must sum to 1 over the measurements of each line. A full scan measures every line twice and weights each view with half its angle step; a parallel-beam scan over half the circle measures every line once and weights each view with its full angle step.

source
CTOperators.ArrayRedundancy — Type
ArrayRedundancy(weights, views)

A redundancy weighting given by arrays, for weights computed elsewhere: weights the weight of each data element, an array of size (nu, nv, nviews) (the data size of the geometry), where a dimension of length 1 stands for all cells or views along it (as in broadcasting; (nu, 1, nviews): the same weight for every row), and views a vector with the weight of each view (see AbstractRedundancy for how both enter, and for the condition they must meet). Only the sizes are checked: the weights must suit the scan.

weights is moved to the device of the plan with adapt(storage, weights) and keeps its element type (on devices without Float64, pass Float32 weights); the kernel converts each weight to the element type of the plan.

Example

# a full scan with 360 equal steps as arrays: weight 1 for all data elements, half the angle step per view
geometry = circular_cone_beam(; angles=range(0, 2pi, 361)[1:360], dso=500, dsd=1000, nu=256, nv=128)
plan = FDK(image, geometry; redundancy=ArrayRedundancy(ones(1, 1, 1), fill(pi / 360, 360)))
source
CTOperators.FullScan — Type
FullScan()

The redundancy weighting of a full scan (the default of FDK): the views go once around the circle (parallel beams: half or once around), so every line is measured equally often. The angle step of a view is half the angle between its two neighbors, the first and the last view taking the gap that closes the circle as the missing neighbor; each view is weighted with half its angle step (every line measured twice), parallel beams over half the circle with the full step (every line measured once). Uneven angle steps are allowed; the gap must be at most 1.5 times the largest step. All data elements have the same weight.

source
CTOperators.ParkerWeights — Type
ParkerWeights()

The redundancy weighting of a short scan (Parker 1982): views over at least half the circle plus the fan angle, in order, with angle steps that may differ. Lines near the start and the end of the scan are measured twice, from views about half a circle apart; their two data elements get weights that add up to 1 and fall smoothly to 0 at the first and the last view. A data element at the angle β of its view (from the first view, along the orbit) and the fan angle γ of its cell (the angle between its ray and the central ray, within the plane of the orbit) gets the weight

  • sin²(π β / (4 (Δ - γ))) for 0 ≤ β < 2 (Δ - γ),
  • 1 up to β = π - 2γ,
  • sin²(π (π + 2Δ - β) / (4 (Δ + γ))) up to β = π + 2Δ,

where π + 2Δ is the angle between the first and the last view. Parker's weights take Δ as half the fan angle, the shortest scan; for longer scans, up to one turn, Δ = (span - π) / 2 keeps the weights of a line adding up to 1 (Wesarg et al. 2002, Eq. 15). Each view is weighted with its angle step in the back projection. A scan must cover at least π plus the fan angle of the detector (twice the largest |γ| of its cells); a parallel-beam scan at least π (no fan: γ = 0).

In a cone beam the weights are those of the fan in the plane of the orbit, for every row; Wesarg et al. (2002) state that the weights carry over to the circular cone beam with Feldkamp's approximation. With a detector offset, the columns beyond the symmetric part of the fan see lines whose second measurement falls off the detector; their weights assume both measurements. With data that are not smooth at the first and the last view (an object that reaches out of the field of view), the reconstruction shows streaks; a slightly longer scan avoids the jump of the weights at the edges of the fan (Wesarg et al. 2002).

References

  • D. L. Parker, "Optimal short scan convolution reconstruction for fanbeam CT", Medical Physics 9(2), 254–257, 1982 (Eq. 12; the denominator of its third case must read δ + α, as Eq. 9 requires).
  • S. Wesarg, M. Ebert, T. Bortfeld, "Parker weights revisited", Medical Physics 29(3), 372–378,

CTOperators.CircularScan — Type
CircularScan

The circular scan of a geometry as FDK sees it, the input of redundancy_weights. Fields (lengths in the unit of the geometry, angles in radians):

  • axis: unit vector along the rotation axis (the direction of the detector columns)
  • angles: the angle of each view about the axis, unwrapped: strictly increasing or decreasing, the first in (-π, π]. For cone beams the angle of the source, for parallel beams that of the ray direction.
  • dso, dsd: per view the distance from the source to the axis and to the detector along the central ray (both 1 for parallel beams)
  • central: 3 × nviews, the unit vector of the central ray of each view, from the source toward the axis and perpendicular to it (for parallel beams the ray direction)
