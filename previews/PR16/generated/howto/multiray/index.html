Use Several Rays per Cell

A detector cell measures the radiation that falls onto its whole area. The projectors trace one ray per cell, through its center. When the cells are large compared to the voxels, the back projection of such rays has gaps (Miao 2013, p. 29). This guide shows the effect on a small example and how MultiRay traces several rays per cell instead. Several Rays per Cell explains how the rays are placed and weighted, and Comparing Projectors when it matters.

using CTOperators, ImagePhantoms, RegularizedLeastSquares, LinearOperatorCollection, CairoMakie
using ImageGeoms: axis
support_figure (generic function with 1 method)

The example

A 2D Shepp-Logan phantom of 128 × 128 pixels and a parallel-beam scan with 180 views (projection directions). The detector cells are 2 pixels wide; u runs along a detector row, v across the rows. In a cone beam, the size to compare with the voxels is the cell size at the object, the cell size divided by the magnification dsd / dso.

n = 128
image = ImageGeom(; dims=(n, n), deltas=(1.0, 1.0))
x_true = Float32.(phantom(axis(image, 1), axis(image, 2), shepp_logan(SheppLoganToft(); fovs=(n, n)), 2))
geometry = parallel_beam(; angles=range(0, pi, 181)[1:180], nu=96, nv=1, du=2.0);

The data average the line integrals over the width of each cell. They are simulated on a 4 times finer image with 16 rays per cell, so that they do not come from any of the models compared below:

fine = ImageGeom(; dims=(4n, 4n), deltas=(0.25, 0.25))
x_fine = Float32.(phantom(axis(fine, 1), axis(fine, 2), shepp_logan(SheppLoganToft(); fovs=(n, n)), 2))
y = JosephProjector(fine, MultiRay(geometry, RegularPoints(16, 1))) * x_fine;

The reconstructions use 40 iterations of CGNR (conjugate gradients on the normal equations):

function reconstruct(op)
  solver = createLinearSolver(CGNR, op; AHA=normalOperator(op), iterations=40)
  reshape(solve!(solver, vec(y)), n, n)
end;

One ray per cell

Without MultiRay, each data element (one number of the projection data: one cell in one view) is one ray, through the center of its cell. Positions on the detector are counted in cells: cell 2 has its center at position 2 and its edges at 1.5 and 2.5. With cells twice as wide as the pixels, the rays of a view miss every other line of pixels (left; gray: pixels, orange: rays, blue: cells):

From view to view the rays shift and miss other pixels, but the misses do not cancel. The column sums of the system matrix show it: colsums is the back projection of data that are all ones, in every pixel the summed weights of all rays, and with even coverage it would be flat.

op = SiddonProjector(image, geometry)
x_center = reconstruct(op);

Several rays per cell

MultiRay wraps the geometry and traces several rays per cell, here at the centers of 4 equal subcells along u (right in the figure above). The value of a cell is the mean of the line integrals of its rays; the data keep their size.

op_multi = SiddonProjector(image, MultiRay(geometry, RegularPoints(4, 1)))
x_multi = reconstruct(op_multi);

The titles give the variation, the standard deviation relative to the mean inside a circle of radius 60 pixels; the window is ±20 % around the mean. With one ray per cell the column sums show rings and a cross around the center, with peaks of about ±30 %. With 4 rays per cell the variation drops by a factor of about 30, and the peaks to about ±1 %.

The reconstructions, in the color window 0 to 0.4, which shows the soft tissue and clips the skull, with the relative error $\|x - x_\text{true}\| / \|x_\text{true}\|$:

With 4 rays per cell the grainy pattern inside the head and between the ellipses goes away, and the error drops from 0.33 to 0.27. The streaks outside the head remain. On this example, 16 rays give 0.27 as well, and the gap depends on when the iterations stop: with 10 iterations the errors are 0.35 and 0.28, with 200 they are 0.33 and 0.32.

JosephProjector takes a MultiRay the same way. The rays of a 2D image stay in the image plane, so the points are spread along u only (RegularPoints(4, 1); RegularPoints(4) would be an error). For a 3D image, RegularPoints(4) places 4 × 4 = 16 rays per cell, and the time grows accordingly.

Other point strategies

Besides RegularPoints, the rays of a cell can end at random offsets, fixed by a seed (default 0):

  • JitteredPoints(4, 1; seed=1): each regular point moved to a random position within its subcell;
  • RandomPoints(4; seed=1): 4 random points anywhere in the cell;
  • GaussianPoints(4, 0.3; seed=1): 4 random points, normally distributed around the cell center with a standard deviation of 0.3 cells, which can also land in neighboring cells (blur).

Points(offsets) takes given offsets, a 2 × K matrix in cell units, the same in every cell. How the strategies compare on the data above:

op_jittered = SiddonProjector(image, MultiRay(geometry, JitteredPoints(4, 1; seed=1)))
op_random = SiddonProjector(image, MultiRay(geometry, RandomPoints(4; seed=1)))
x_jittered, x_random = reconstruct(op_jittered), reconstruct(op_random);

On these data, whose cells average evenly over their width, regular points give the smallest error of the three, 4 jittered rays lie close to one ray per cell, and 4 random rays above it. This is one example; other detectors and data can behave differently.

Ray weights

The rays of a cell can also count differently, e.g. for a detector response that falls off towards the cell edges. The ray weights are normalized per cell:

op_weighted = SiddonProjector(image, MultiRay(geometry, RegularPoints(4, 1); weights=GaussianWeights(0.4)));

On the data above the error is 0.27, as with uniform ray weights.

Cost and devices

  • The time grows with the number of rays per cell: 4 rays take about 4 times as long. Jittered points and Gaussian ray weights added nothing measurable on a GPU (see the benchmarks). No extra memory is needed.
  • MultiRay works on every device (storage=CuArray etc.), and the random positions are the same on all of them, in the forward and the back projection, and for subset(op, views).
  • Masks work as on plain geometries: a masked data element skips all its rays.
  • Only detector geometries have cells: a RayList cannot be wrapped.

References

  • C. Miao, "Comparative studies of different system models for iterative CT image reconstruction", master's thesis, Wake Forest University, 2013.
