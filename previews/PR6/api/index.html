API

The sections follow the parts of the package: the image geometry, the projection geometries and their rays, the generic operator interface, and one section per projector.

Image geometry

Images are described by ImageGeom from ImageGeoms.jl, re-exported by CTOperators.jl. Voxel i has its center at ((i - 1) - (n - 1)/2 - offset) * Δ per dimension; a 2D image is treated as a slice at z = 0 that is 1 length unit thick. Lengths have no fixed unit: image and projection geometry must use the same one.

Projection geometries

Interface

CTOperators.AbstractDetectorGeometry — Type
AbstractDetectorGeometry{T} <: AbstractProjectionGeometry{T}

Geometries of a source (or ray direction) and a flat or curved detector per view; the projection data has size (nu, nv, nviews). Besides the rays they describe the detector cells.

Interface (in addition to that of AbstractProjectionGeometry):

  • nviews(g), detectorsize(g) = (nu, nv)
  • subset(g, views): the geometry of a subset of the views
source
CTOperators.AbstractProjectionGeometry — Type
AbstractProjectionGeometry{T}

Supertype of acquisition geometries: where the rays run and the shape of the projection data. T is the element type of the stored coordinates.

Interface:

  • datasize(g): size of the projection data, e.g. (nu, nv, nviews)
  • ray(g, i) -> (a, b): ray i (linear index into the projection data) as two points SVector{3,T}; callable inside kernels
  • raytype(g): RaySegment() if the ray is the segment from a to b, RayLine() if it is the whole line through a and b

Geometries are moved to another device and converted to another element type with adapt of Adapt.jl (using Adapt), e.g. adapt(CuArray{Float32}, g). Lengths have no fixed unit; they must be in the unit of the ImageGeom they are used with.

source
CTOperators.RayLine — Type
RayLine()

raytype of geometries whose ray i is the whole line through the two points of ray(g, i), e.g. parallel beams. Only the part inside the volume contributes.

source
CTOperators.RaySegment — Type
RaySegment()

raytype of geometries whose ray i is the segment between the two points of ray(g, i), e.g. from the source to a detector pixel.

Geometry types

CTOperators.RayList — Type
RayList(starts, ends; datasize=(size(starts, 2),))

Arbitrary rays given by their start and end points (columns of the 3 × nrays matrices starts and ends). Ray i is the segment from starts[:, i] to ends[:, i]. datasize gives the projection data a shape, e.g. a sinogram: ray i is element i of the data array in column-major order.

RayList(g::AbstractProjectionGeometry)

The rays of a geometry with RaySegment rays, computed on the device of g (geometries with RayLine rays, such as parallel beams, have no end points and throw an error).

source
CTOperators.ConeBeam — Type
ConeBeam(source, center, u, v; nu, nv)

Cone beam with a flat detector of nu × nv cells and an arbitrary trajectory, given per view k (columns of 3 × nviews matrices):

  • source[:, k]: position of the source
  • center[:, k]: center of the detector
  • u[:, k], v[:, k]: vectors from one detector cell to the next along the rows (u) and the columns (v); their lengths are the cell sizes

The center of cell (iu, iv) is center + (iu − (nu + 1)/2) u + (iv − (nv + 1)/2) v, and ray (iu, iv, k) runs from the source to this point. The projection data has size (nu, nv, nviews).

circular_cone_beam and helical_cone_beam construct the common trajectories.

source
CTOperators.ArcConeBeam — Type
ArcConeBeam(source, center, u, v; nu, nv)

Cone beam with a curved detector of nu × nv cells on a cylinder about the source, as in clinical CT scanners, and an arbitrary trajectory, given per view k (columns of 3 × nviews matrices):

  • source[:, k]: position of the source, on the axis of the detector cylinder (the axis runs along v)
  • center[:, k]: center of the detector; its distance from the axis is the radius of the cylinder
  • u[:, k]: the direction of the detector rows at the center (tangent to the arc); its length is the cell size along the arc
  • v[:, k]: vector from one detector row to the next along the cylinder axis; its length is the cell size

Cell (iu, iv) sits at the fan angle γ = (iu − (nu + 1)/2) |u| / r from the center, with r the radius, and is offset by (iv − (nv + 1)/2) v along the cylinder axis; ray (iu, iv, k) runs from the source to it. The projection data has size (nu, nv, nviews). u must be orthogonal to v and to center − source (checked for CPU arrays).

circular_cone_beam and helical_cone_beam with detector=:arc construct the common trajectories.

source
CTOperators.ParallelBeam — Type
ParallelBeam(direction, center, u, v; nu, nv)

Parallel beam with a flat detector of nu × nv cells, given per view k (columns of 3 × nviews matrices):

  • direction[:, k]: direction of the rays (any nonzero length)
  • center[:, k]: the center of the detector; since the rays are whole lines, its position along direction does not matter
  • u[:, k], v[:, k]: vectors from one detector cell to the next along the rows (u) and the columns (v); their lengths are the cell sizes

Ray (iu, iv, k) is the whole line through the center of cell (iu, iv) (see ConeBeam) along direction[:, k] (RayLine). The projection data has size (nu, nv, nviews).

parallel_beam constructs the common rotation about the z axis.

source

Trajectories

CTOperators.circular_cone_beam — Method
circular_cone_beam(; angles, dso, dsd, nu, nv, du, dv=du, offset_u=0, offset_v=0, source_z=0,
                   detector=:flat)

Cone-beam geometry of a source on a circle of radius dso about the z axis and a detector at distance dsd from the source, nu × nv cells of size du × dv, one view per angle in angles (radians, source counterclockwise from the y axis). At angle β = 0 the source is on the +y axis, the detector rows run along +x and the columns along +z.

detector=:flat gives a flat detector (ConeBeam), detector=:arc a detector curved on a cylinder about the source (ArcConeBeam), with du the cell size along the arc at distance dsd (an angle of du / dsd per cell) and offset_u shifting the cells along the arc.

source_z is the height of source and detector center, a number or one value per view; with source_z varying the trajectory is no longer circular (see helical_cone_beam).

offset_u and offset_v shift the detector within its plane, in cells: the cell positions along u are ((0:nu-1) .- (nu - 1)/2 .- offset_u) .* du, so a positive offset moves the detector towards −u (−v). Lengths are in the unit of the ImageGeom the geometry is used with.

source
CTOperators.helical_cone_beam — Method
helical_cone_beam(; angles, pitch, z0=0, kwargs...)

ConeBeam geometry (with detector=:arc an ArcConeBeam) of a helical trajectory: as circular_cone_beam (same keywords), with source and detector moving along z by pitch (a length) per turn, z = z0 + pitch (β − β₁)/2π for view angle β (β₁ = angles[1]).

An object that translates along the rotation axis with speed s during a circular scan sees this geometry with pitch = −s · (time per turn).

source
CTOperators.parallel_beam — Method
parallel_beam(; angles, nu, nv, du, dv=du, offset_u=0, offset_v=0)

ParallelBeam geometry rotating about the z axis, nu × nv cells of size du × dv, one view per angle in angles (radians). At angle ϕ the rays run along (−sin ϕ, cos ϕ, 0) and the detector rows along (cos ϕ, sin ϕ, 0); the detector is centered on the rotation axis, its columns along +z. This matches the parallel-beam coordinates (u, v, ϕ) of radon in ImagePhantoms.jl.

offset_u and offset_v shift the detector within its plane, in cells: the cell positions along u are ((0:nu-1) .- (nu - 1)/2 .- offset_u) .* du, so a positive offset moves the detector towards −u (−v). Lengths are in the unit of the ImageGeom the geometry is used with.

Operators

Generic interface

CTOperators.AbstractCTOperator — Type
AbstractCTOperator{T}

Supertype of the CT projection operators of CTOperators.jl. T is the real element type of image and projection data. An operator is created once and applied many times: op * x is the forward projection, adjoint(op) * y the back projection (its adjoint; transpose(op) is the same). op * x returns an array on the operator's device, a vector for a vector x and an array of the data shape otherwise; mul! accepts both shapes.

With LinearOperators.jl loaded, LinearOperators.storage_type(op) is the vector type of the operator's device, so that operators of LinearOperatorCollection.jl take CT operators, e.g. the normal operator normalOperator(op) for the solvers of RegularizedLeastSquares.jl.

source
CTOperators.AbstractProjector — Type
AbstractProjector{T,G<:AbstractProjectionGeometry} <: AbstractCTOperator{T}

Supertype of the projectors of this package: operators between an image on an ImageGeom and projection data of a geometry of type G (stored with its arrays on the device).

Interface:

  • geometry(op)::G, image_geometry(op)::ImageGeom
  • similar_projector(op, geometry): the same kind of projector, with the same image geometry and options, for another geometry (used by subset and adapt)
  • the methods of AbstractCTOperator
source
CTOperators.AdjointCTOperator — Type
AdjointCTOperator(op)

The adjoint (back projection) of op, created by adjoint(op); applying it calls backward!(x, op, y).

source
CTOperators.colsums — Method
colsums(op::AbstractCTOperator)

adjoint(op) * ones: for projectors the sensitivity image, the summed weights of all rays per voxel. A normalization of SART/SIRT/OS-EM-type methods.

source
CTOperators.geometry — Function
geometry(op::AbstractProjector)

The projection geometry of op, with its arrays on the device of the operator.

source
CTOperators.image_geometry — Function
image_geometry(op::AbstractProjector)

The ImageGeom the operator was built for.

source
CTOperators.imagesize — Function
imagesize(op)

Size of the operator's input: the image for a projector (2D or 3D, as its ImageGeom), the projection data for its adjoint.

source
CTOperators.rowsums — Method
rowsums(op::AbstractCTOperator)

op * ones: for projectors the length of each ray inside the image (exactly for Siddon's method, approximately for Joseph's). A normalization of SART/SIRT-type methods.

source
CTOperators.subset — Method
subset(op::AbstractProjector, views)

The projector of the views views of op (detector geometries; a vector or range of view indices), for ordered-subset methods. It shares the image geometry and options with op.

source
CTOperators.LaunchConfig — Type
LaunchConfig(; workgroupsize=nothing, tile=nothing)

How a projector launches its kernels (forward and back projection), for tuning on a particular device. Pass it to a projector with the keyword launch.

  • workgroupsize: the number of threads (work-items) per workgroup, nothing for the choice of KernelAbstractions.jl.
  • tile: (tu, tv) to let the threads of a workgroup take the rays of tiles of tu × tv detector cells instead of consecutive cells of a detector row (detector geometries only; ray lists keep their order), nothing for data order. Tiles at the edges of the detector are padded with idle threads; a workgroup size of tu * tv gives one tile per workgroup.

Without launch (or with launch=nothing) a projector takes the defaults of its device, also after adapt to another device: on GPUs workgroups of 64 and tiles of 8 × 8 cells, on the CPU data order and the backend's workgroups.

The GPU defaults were tuned on one AMD GPU; tune them for yours. See the explanation page Workgroups and Ray Order on GPUs in the documentation.

Example

using CUDA
launch = LaunchConfig(; workgroupsize=128, tile=(16, 8))
op = JosephProjector(image, geometry; storage=CuArray, launch)
source

Siddon

CTOperators.SiddonProjector — Type
SiddonProjector(image::ImageGeom, geometry; eltype=Float32, storage=Array, launch=nothing)

Projector with Siddon's method: the weight of a voxel for a ray is the exact length of the ray inside the voxel (Siddon 1985), computed incrementally as in Jacobs et al. 1998. Works with every AbstractProjectionGeometry. The back projection is the exact adjoint.

Keywords:

  • eltype is the element type of the computation and of the stored geometry.
  • storage is the array type that selects the device (Array, CuArray, ROCArray, …). Image and projection data passed to the operator must live on that device.
  • launch is a LaunchConfig to tune the kernel launches for a device, nothing for the defaults of the device.

Example

image = ImageGeom(; dims=(128, 128, 64), deltas=(1, 1, 1))
geometry = circular_cone_beam(; angles=range(0, 2pi, 361)[1:360], dso=500, dsd=1000,
                              nu=256, nv=128, du=1, dv=1)
op = SiddonProjector(image, geometry)
x = rand(Float32, 128, 128, 64)
y = op * x                 # forward projection, size (256, 128, 360)
z = adjoint(op) * y        # back projection

References:

  • R. L. Siddon, "Fast calculation of the exact radiological path for a three-dimensional CT array", Med. Phys. 12(2), 252–255, 1985.
  • F. Jacobs, E. Sundermann, B. De Sutter, M. Christiaens, I. Lemahieu, "A fast algorithm to calculate the exact radiological path through a pixel or voxel space", J. Comput. Inf. Technol. 6(1), 89–94, 1998.
source

Joseph

CTOperators.JosephProjector — Type
JosephProjector(image::ImageGeom, geometry; eltype=Float32, storage=Array, launch=nothing)

Projector with Joseph's method (Joseph 1982): the image is interpolated bilinearly within each layer of voxels perpendicular to the axis the ray runs most along (its principal axis), and the line integral is the sum over the layers of the interpolated value times the length of the ray in the layer. Compared with SiddonProjector:

  • the image model is smooth, so the projection changes continuously when a ray moves;
  • the rays of a view cover the voxels more evenly, except near 45°, where the two methods give similar weights;
  • the projection is exact for images that are linear functions of the position rather than for voxel images.

Works with every AbstractProjectionGeometry. The back projection is the exact adjoint.

Outside the volume the image is zero; across the ray it fades to zero up to half a voxel beyond the faces of the volume. If a segment starts or ends inside the volume, only the part of the end layer it actually crosses is counted.

Keywords:

  • eltype is the element type of the computation and of the stored geometry.
  • storage is the array type that selects the device (Array, CuArray, ROCArray, …). Image and projection data passed to the operator must live on that device.
  • launch is a LaunchConfig to tune the kernel launches for a device, nothing for the defaults of the device.

Example

image = ImageGeom(; dims=(128, 128, 64), deltas=(1, 1, 1))
geometry = circular_cone_beam(; angles=range(0, 2pi, 361)[1:360], dso=500, dsd=1000,
                              nu=256, nv=128, du=1, dv=1)
op = JosephProjector(image, geometry)
x = rand(Float32, 128, 128, 64)
y = op * x                 # forward projection, size (256, 128, 360)
z = adjoint(op) * y        # back projection

Reference:

  • P. M. Joseph, "An improved algorithm for reprojecting rays through pixel images", IEEE Trans. Med. Imaging 1(3), 192–196, 1982.

Internals

For developers of operators: the kernel form of the image grid, clipping of rays and the functions each operator implements.

CTOperators.VoxelGrid — Type
VoxelGrid{T}(ig::ImageGeom)

The voxel grid of an ImageGeom in the form the kernels use: three dimensions (a 2D ImageGeom becomes a volume with nz = 1 and voxel size 1 along z, centered at z = 0), element type T, no units, no mask. first is the world coordinate of the center of voxel (1, 1, 1); voxel (i, j, k) has its center at first + ((i, j, k) .- 1) .* deltas. Negative deltas (flipped axes, allowed by ImageGeoms) are kept.

Units of the ImageGeom are stripped; they must be the units of the projection geometry.

source
CTOperators.backward! — Function
backward!(x, op::AbstractCTOperator, y)

Back projection (the adjoint) of the projection data y into the image x, overwriting x. Implemented by every operator; users call mul!(x, adjoint(op), y) or adjoint(op) * y.

source
CTOperators.bounds — Method

Corners of the box covered by the voxels (outer faces, not centers), lo .<= hi.

source
CTOperators.clip — Method
clip(p, d, dims, ::RaySegment | ::RayLine) -> (amin, amax)

Parameters of the part of the ray p + α d (voxel coordinates) inside the volume of size dims: the box [1/2, n + 1/2] per dimension, intersected with α ∈ [0, 1] for segments. The ray misses the volume if amin >= amax.

clip(p, d, lo, hi, ::RaySegment | ::RayLine) -> (amin, amax)

Parameters of the part of the ray p + α d inside the box [lo, hi], intersected with α ∈ [0, 1] for segments.

source
CTOperators.to_index — Method
to_index(g::VoxelGrid, p)

Continuous voxel coordinates of the world point p: voxel (i, j, k) covers [i − 1/2, i + 1/2) × …, its center has the integer coordinates (i, j, k). In these coordinates the voxels are unit cubes with positive orientation, whatever the sign of deltas.
